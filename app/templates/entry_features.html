On what basis is each path actually confirming an entry? Every feature below was available in smc_data or in the evaluator's own scope at the moment the signal was created, and the evaluators read almost none of it. The feature sets differ per path on purpose — the paths are not blind in the same place, and a list chosen for one measures nothing on another. A small set of these readings is now applied: see the live rules below.

Live entry-quality rules

The consuming half of this lane. A rule here runs in the scanner after the confidence floor, so a suppression is always a candidate that would otherwise have emitted — the count means “signals this gate cost us”, not “signals that were dying anyway”. A rule ships live only when enforcing it invents no number: it repairs a filter the engine already computes and then discards. A rule whose threshold is a judgement stays in shadow until this page has the evidence to move it. “Their result” colours the other way round on purpose. It is the performance of rows the rule would have removed, so a negative number is the rule looking right. It is measured only on rows that emitted and closed — a rule already enforcing does not produce them, which is why the Suppressed column carries no result and never will. An enforcing rule starves its own evidence, so every suppression is stamped into the suppression audit instead and forward-measured on real candles: Strategy Lab → gate audit is where an enforcing rule keeps earning or losing its place.

Unknown is not a pass. A feature that did not compute makes its rule abstain — deliberately, because a fail-closed rule here would kill the whole feed the moment an upstream went dark, and that failure is indistinguishable from a quiet market. The cost is that an inert rule looks like a working one on every count except this column, which is why it is a column and why an enforcing rule that abstains on 80%+ of its own population is badged blind and pages the liveness watchdog.

The cap is a blast radius, not a filter. Neither rule's rejection volume had ever been measured before it went live. Once the gate has rejected more than its configured fraction of recent decisions it degrades to shadow — it keeps stamping and stops suppressing — until the window recovers. That state is counted above rather than left to look like a quiet market, and it is order-dependent by construction: the honest cost of a bound that could not be computed in advance.

Read the n and the kept-fraction before the Δ. A rule that keeps 95% of the book has not been tested by this window whatever its Δ says, and a rule whose Δ rests on three trades is not a finding — the window that prompted this work had 46 MVRTP signals, 19 tested cells, and exactly one cleared 95% in the backwards direction against a ~62% chance of a spurious hit. Unknown rows are counted apart on purpose: a rule cannot be credited with an outcome it could not have seen, and folding them into “keep” is how a filter takes credit for rows it never filtered.

{{ cells_drawn }} cells drawn on this view. “Best of {{ cells_drawn }}” is not a fact about the winner until that number is on screen — the top row of a table this long beats a coin flip by construction. Treat the leader as a candidate for more evidence, never as a result; FAILED_AUCTION_RECLAIM read +0.846R on three rows and a bootstrap CI of [−1.00, +2.00], and the promotion request followed within the day.

tp1_r_multiple is the row to read first, and it is not a candidate filter like the others — it is chosen by the evaluator, exact at stamp time, and it bounds what every other row can achieve. In the 2026-08-01 dark window TREND_PULLBACK_EMA ran a median designed R:R of 0.79 (TP1 nearer than the stop), needing a 54% win rate to break even and posting 35% over 17 decided rows.

That is not an argument for raising TP1 — it was written here as though it were, and testing it against the same data showed the opposite. Simulated on the 2026-08-01 11:00 window (55 decided rows), flooring TP1 at 1.0R takes the book from −0.081R to between −0.186R and −0.404R; at 1.5R, to between −0.245R and −0.536R — and it reproduces on the earlier export, so the direction is not one window's artefact. The winners barely clear their current targets (TPE's hit at a median 0.59R against a 0.89R peak) and only 27% of decided trades ever moved 1R in our favour. Median favourable excursion across the book is 0.53R. The low target is not the defect; it is what harvests a move this small.

So read this column as a description of what the book can earn, not as a lever: it says the trades are small relative to the risk they are sized for, which points at entry quality and at loss size. Any change to it is a TP/SL-shape decision and therefore owner-sign-off; this page only makes it visible.
