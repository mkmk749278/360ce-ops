Firestore — the read allowance, and what it becomes at 1,000 members

Why this page exists

On 2026-09-02 at 00:41 UTC Firestore answered RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED: Quota exceeded. on 53,000 document reads against a 50,000/day allowance — on 25 writes. Every Firestore-backed path failed together: the keystore (so list_active_uids returned empty and every signal fanned out to zero users), the kill switch (so POST /api/kill-switch 503'd — the emergency stop and the thing it stops failing at once), the runtime tunables and the dispatch log.

That budget is a hard ceiling, not a bill. Past it a project whose billing account is not in good standing is refused, so this is a safety surface, not a finance one — which is why it sits beside the container X-rays rather than under diagnostics.

The auto-trade target is 1,000 members. Every per-user read is invisible at one user and linear in subscribers, so nothing in the console, the bill or the census says a word about it until the subscribers arrive — worker_manager's roster scan cost 1,440 reads a day on this account and 1.44 million at the target. The projection below is the engine's, rendered here rather than recomputed: ops ports the engine's arithmetic and does not invent it, and a second implementation of a cost model drifts silently in the flattering direction.

Invalidation channel

The three control documents are no longer re-read on a TTL. A write bumps a Redis generation and every reader drops its cache on the next tick, which is both cheaper and faster than the 5-second TTL it replaced — that TTL equalled MONITOR_POLL_INTERVAL exactly, so the cache could never hit and the kill-switch document alone cost ~34,000 reads a day through two slots.

A dead channel is invisible in the ordinary case, because the defensive 300-second floor still converges — just minutes late. These counters are the only thing that can say a flip is taking the slow path.
