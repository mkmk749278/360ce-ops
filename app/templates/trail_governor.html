Trail governor — the exit mechanism placing real orders

Every other trail page in this dashboard — SAR live, ATR trail live, SAR signals, the Profit tab's bake-off — renders a measurement: where a stop would have been parked. This page renders the stop the engine is actually amending on a live Binance account, on every closed bar, for the users who opted in.

Two switches are required and they are not the same switch. The master switch below is engine-wide; the mechanism is chosen per user (exit_mechanism). Master ON with nobody opted in governs nothing — which is the intended state for everyone except the account running the test.

The measured edge over the current exit does not exclude zero. On the 2026-08-10 window SAR read +2.687pp per arm against the live SL/TP exit, and −0.152pp once the two campaigns carrying the book (BMTUSDT, COOKIEUSDT — 15 of 35 arms) came out; the campaign-clustered 95% CI was [−0.59, +4.87]pp. This is a canary on one account, not a rollout.

Sweep counters

exits is the mechanism's own exit — SAR flipped, or the chandelier's level is already past the close — closed at market and the parked stop retired with it. Counted apart from replaced because they are opposite events: one moves protection, the other ends the trade. Before 2026-08-11 there was no such leg at all, so a flip produced a level the exchange can only answer with -2021 and the position sat on its last pre-flip stop while the measured arm had already booked the trade closed.

orphan_cancel is the one to watch: a replacement stop is live but the cancel of the one it superseded failed, so two are resting. That is over-protected, not dangerous — the governor's stop is reduceOnly for the position's own size, so whichever level triggers first closes the position and the exchange rejects the remainder rather than opening or flipping anything, and the cancel is retried next bar. It does not clear itself. This page said until 2026-08-11 that Binance cancels the other one once the position closes; it does not for a conditional stop, and PROMUSDT's reduce-only stop outlived its position by 28 minutes on the owner's own account. A non-zero count here is an order somebody has to retire.

place_failed is the safe failure — nothing was given up, the previous stop is still exactly where it was — but it is not a benign one: a rejection that repeats is a handover that will never happen, so read it beside handovers and check the rejections below. retry_deferred is the governor declining to re-ask the exchange about a bar it has already refused; the level is fixed for the bar, so the answer would be the same. exit_failed is not deferred that way — a market close that failed on a transient is worth re-asking, and leaving a position in a mechanism that has said exit is the state the exit leg exists to remove.

Traded history — every governed position that has closed

The counters above say the machine is turning; they cannot say what it made. Until 2026-08-11 that was the whole story: this page published handovers and replaced, SAR live published the shadow, and Track record reads the signal record — so the realized result of a governed exit, the one number an own-capital test exists to produce, landed on no surface in either repo.

This is the record, not a measurement. Every other trail page here renders a counterfactual that can be re-derived by waiting for a fresh window; these are real fills on a real account, and nothing in the engine will produce them again. So it is never reconstructed and never merged with a replayed figure — Track record's rule, arriving at the money leg. It is persisted across deploys, which the first cut of this panel was not: that ring lived in memory, held 40 rows and was destroyed by every restart.

The two fills are never pooled, exactly as on SAR live. Their difference is the cost of confirmation, so there is deliberately no blended average — picking one before that cost is known is choosing the answer. Figures are gross, signed toward the trade, on the same convention as the arm's pnl_level_pct so the two are directly comparable.
