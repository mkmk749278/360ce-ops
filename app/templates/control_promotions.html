How a rule works, and how to read the tables

A promotion removes only the gate the row was carried past. Every other gate above it already said yes, and the router's second layer (correlation lock, cooldowns, concurrency caps, same-direction throttle) still runs below — so a promoted row is not yet a delivered one.

Measuring does not stop. A promoted row is still written to the dark ledger and still walked, marked promoted with its delivery outcome stamped, so the evidence keeps arriving after the decision instead of freezing at the moment it was taken. Promoted rows and dark rows are never pooled anywhere.

Reading order for every table: n, then campaigns, then the average. A cell is not evidence until its row count and its concentration have been read — three rows at a flattering average is how FAILED_AUCTION_RECLAIM got a promotion request within a day of posting +0.846R on a CI of [−1.00, +2.00]. Nothing is sorted by edge, for the same reason, and each table prints how many cells it drew from.
