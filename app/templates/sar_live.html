This is not a replay. Every arm here was advanced inside the engine's monitor loop while the trade was open, so an open row carries the stop the mechanism would have parked right now — not a level reconstructed after the outcome was known. That is the one thing neither SAR signals (a 48h replay) nor Dark signals (a kline replay after the engine already closed the trade) can show, and it is what a rollout decision actually needs.

The mechanism. At generation, if SAR already agrees with the signal it governs from bar one and the original SL and TP1 are never used — the trade exits when SAR flips. If SAR opposes, the original SL and TP1 stay live until SAR comes onside; at that moment they are cancelled and SAR takes over. If SAR never comes onside, the trade closes on its original stop, or in full at TP1. Measured on 5m and 15m as independent arms on the same signals, so the timeframe question and the mechanism question share one window.

These arms place no orders and change no exit. Live exits still run on the signal's SL/TP and the FSM. Nothing on this page has touched anyone's capital.

Two fills, and why neither is called “the” result

“Close at market when SAR flips” leaves no resting stop between bars, which would breach the never-a-naked-position rule the moment it went live — so the arm models a stop parked at the SAR level and amended each bar, and records both prices: Fill @level is that stop being touched intrabar (a gap through it fills at the bar's open — worse, never better); Fill @confirm is waiting for the bar to close and exiting at market. Confirm cost is the difference. That number has never been measured anywhere in this system, and picking one fill before it is known would be choosing the answer rather than measuring it.

R divides by the SL distance at entry even on arms where SAR governed and that stop was never used, so a number here is comparable with the edge matrix and Track record. Figures are gross — no fees, no funding — so a row reading flat is a real cost live.

Was the SAR stop inside the risk the trade was sized for?

When SAR agrees at entry it governs and the signal's own SL is never used, so an arm can end up carrying more risk than the evaluator sized the trade for. The first two live arms did exactly that on one signal — MUUUSDT SHORT, designed SL 3.00%, 5m SAR at 1.60% and 15m SAR at 3.77%. R divides by the SL distance at entry, so a wider-stop arm mechanically produces bigger losses per stop-out: pooled, "the exit timed badly" and "the exit risked more" would arrive as one number wearing the other's name. Split here so they don't.
