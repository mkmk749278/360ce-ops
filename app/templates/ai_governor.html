AI Trade Governor — what it decided, and what it refused

What this lane is

An asynchronous critic attached to already-open positions. The deterministic FSM places the trade; this asks a model, on the signal's own bar clock, whether reality still supports the premise — and it may answer only MAINTAIN, ADJUST_TP, ADJUST_SL or PANIC_CLOSE. It never emits a price: the engine builds a menu of pre-validated levels and the model returns a key, so a hallucinated choice is bounded by a menu we wrote.

Keyed on signals, not positions — one verdict fans out to every user in the trade, so the model cost is flat in members. The apply path is not: one PANIC_CLOSE at 1,000 members is ~1,000 Firestore reads and ~5,000 signed Binance calls in a burst, from the IP that got rate-limited on 2026-09-01. That is why it carries a second, separate budget.

Read the floor as a reading, not a constant. It tracks the monitor loop, and the loop moves: measured 2026-09-06 at 10.83s on one window and 9.57s about an hour later, entirely because the achieved sweep interval fell from 7.98s to 6.97s — a swing wider than the headroom either reading showed. So a bound sitting within a second of this figure is not reliably passing or reliably failing; which verdicts survive is decided by loop jitter. That is consistent with the historical 41.7% aged-out rate and it is the reason to watch the worst interval above rather than the p50 alone. One window is not a verdict on the bound.

A verdict is stamped at the sweep tick that launches its request, the model answers some seconds later, and the queue is not drained until the next sweep — so the floor is the round trip plus one sweep interval and nothing can be younger. The two terms have opposite fixes: the round trip moves with the provider, the batch size or the bound; the sweep interval moves with the drain cadence and no model change touches it. MONITOR_POLL_INTERVAL is the sleep the loop asks for, not the period it achieves — the figure above is measured. Every number here is the engine's own; ops computes none of it.

The bound is derived from the loop, not configured: the configured number is a floor, and the enforced one is that floor against the slowest recent tick ×1.5, capped at 60s. A verdict is queued in one tick and drained in the next, so a fixed bound smaller than the tick refuses everything — which is what 7-of-8 aged out meant, on a loop measured at 7–20s against a 10s constant. Both numbers render because a reader needs to know which one binds. This paragraph described a derivation that did not exist until 2026-09-08. Ops has rendered verdict_max_age_effective_sec and observed_tick_sec since the panel shipped and the engine sent neither, so the page fell to its “not measured yet” branch under a sentence promising the opposite — while the enforced number stayed the flat constant and sat 4.1s below its own floor, where no verdict could pass it. verdict_age_floor had published that reading since #1015 and nothing consumed it. Against the engine's own exit — paired on one row, not two populations

This is the card that answers "does the governor beat what we do now", and it is a different question from the Scorecard above. That panel compares the signals the governor wanted to touch against the ones it left alone — a selection statistic, and it is labelled as one. Read as an endorsement it points the wrong way: the only arm the model ever chooses is ADJUST_SL, the menu is tighter-only, and most of the touched population were winners. Tightening a stop on a winner can only clip it.

Here every arm walks two exits over one set of bars under one set of fill rules: the geometry a verdict edited, and the geometry the evaluator shipped, frozen when the arm opened. Their difference is that signal's own delta, so there is no population to select and market conditions are removed rather than averaged over.
