These are real signals, not counterfactuals. Each row went through the whole scanner chain — scoring, MTF, min_confidence, the context floors, level_still_in_play, staleness — with only setup_compat or execution overridden for its path. That is what separates this page from the suppression audit, where a gate killed a candidate and a resolver scored it afterwards.

They are not what a user would have seen. The router applies a second layer this lane does not — correlation lock, per-symbol and per-channel cooldown, concurrency caps, same-direction throttle — and drops most of what it dequeues. So a row here means “the scanner was willing to send this”, never “a subscriber would have received this”, and the count over-reports a feed size. Sharing those predicates means changing the router, which is paid-channel routing and ships as its own reviewed change.

Nothing here reached anyone. No channel, no push, no app feed, no order. The engine diverts a dark candidate at the single signal_queue.put site, and the queue is the only route to the router — so it is structural, not a set of skips that could each be forgotten. MOVER_TREND_PULLBACK is excluded: it already owns 64% of the delivered book, and admitting it would make this a second MTP feed.
