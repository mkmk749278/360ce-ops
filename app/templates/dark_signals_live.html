These are real signals, not counterfactuals. Each row went through the whole scanner chain — scoring, MTF, min_confidence, the context floors, level_still_in_play, staleness — with only setup_compat, execution or evaluator overridden for its path. That is what separates this page from the suppression audit, where a gate killed a candidate and a resolver scored it afterwards.

The evaluator override is different in kind, and only sr_flip_long_disabled uses it. The other two are gate-chain rejections; this one fires inside the SR_FLIP evaluator, long before the chain. So the candidate is not published there — it is carried, finishes the evaluator, runs every gate above, and is diverted at the same single enqueue site. Were it published where the disable fires, the first sentence of this page would be false for those rows. SR_FLIP longs have been off since 2026-06-29 on a measured −21.8% (19% win); these rows are how that verdict gets re-earned on outcomes rather than a candidate count, and the long side stays out of the live feed either way.

They are not what a user would have seen. The router applies a second layer this lane does not — correlation lock, per-symbol and per-channel cooldown, concurrency caps, same-direction throttle — and drops most of what it dequeues. So a row here means “the scanner was willing to send this”, never “a subscriber would have received this”, and the count over-reports a feed size. Sharing those predicates means changing the router, which is paid-channel routing and ships as its own reviewed change.

Nothing here reached anyone. No channel, no push, no app feed, no order. The engine diverts a dark candidate at the single signal_queue.put site, and the queue is the only route to the router — so it is structural, not a set of skips that could each be forgotten. MOVER_TREND_PULLBACK is excluded: it already owns 64% of the delivered book, and admitting it would make this a second MTP feed.

These are marks, not results. An unrealized number is what the row is worth at the current price; it is not an outcome and it is kept out of the per-path table below entirely — an unrealized figure in a realized column is how a still-open trade gets read as a finished one. R divides by the engine's stamped SL distance, the same denominator its realized R uses, so a mark here is comparable with a result there; a row whose SL distance the engine did not stamp gets a percentage and no R rather than an invented denominator.

Two populations, and neither is “the” number. The mark on a stalled row is arithmetically fine — entry is fixed and the price is live — but its status is a claim about bars that stopped arriving, so it may have resolved already without the ledger knowing. The two rows above agree while the lane is healthy and diverge exactly when it is not, which is the point of printing both.

Neither column is “the” result. The regular row exits at the stop the evaluator set; the SAR arm cancels that stop the moment SAR comes onside and parks its own. @level is that stop touched intrabar, @confirm is the flip confirmed at the close and exited at market. Their difference is the cost of confirmation, it is never zero, and that is why there is no blended figure here. R against designed risk divides by the SL distance the trade was sized for; R against risk parked divides by the stop SAR actually had out, which has been the wider of the two on most handovers. Both are defensible, so both are printed.

These arms are kept in their own ledger, separate from the live SAR arms. Every arm there belongs to a signal a subscriber received; every arm here belongs to one nobody received. Pooling them would inflate the population an adoption decision reads.
