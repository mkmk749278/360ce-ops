The evaluator override is different in kind, and only sr_flip_long_disabled uses it. The other two are gate-chain rejections; this one fires inside the SR_FLIP evaluator, long before the chain. So the candidate is not published there — it is carried, finishes the evaluator, runs every gate above, and is diverted at the same single enqueue site. Were it published where the disable fires, the first sentence of this page would be false for those rows. They are not what a user would have seen. The router applies a second layer this lane does not — correlation lock, per-symbol and per-channel cooldown, concurrency caps, same-direction throttle — and drops most of what it dequeues. So a row here means “the scanner was willing to send this”, never “a subscriber would have received this”, and the count over-reports a feed size. Sharing those predicates means changing the router, which is paid-channel routing and ships as its own reviewed change.

Nothing here reached anyone. No channel, no push, no app feed, no order. Nothing on this panel is applied to anything. These rows never reached a channel, a push, the app feed or an order, and the exit methods above are replays of what the engine already recorded — no candle is re-walked here and no fill is invented. Fill order comes from the engine's stamps: a ladder level counts only if it was touched on a bar strictly before the one that took out the stop, so a same-bar tie was already resolved against the trade upstream rather than being rounded in its favour here.

The break-even method is the one approximation, and it leans the other way. A stop moved to entry exits at the first retrace through entry after the trigger, which can precede a TP1 this model still books — so that row is optimistic where price ran up, came back through entry, and only then reached TP1. It is exact everywhere else. Ported from the Profit tab deliberately, so “BE stop at +{{ "%g"|format(target_pct) }}%” means the same thing on both pages.

There is no R here. Position sizing is a fixed notional, so a percentage is proportional to money and an R is not — and R would let a method that harvests tight-stop trades outrank one that makes more money. The muted R column in the table below stays as the bridge to the Strategy Lab and is not what a verdict is read from.

Neither column is “the” result. The regular row exits at the stop the evaluator set; the SAR arm cancels that stop the moment SAR comes onside and parks its own. @level is that stop touched intrabar, @confirm is the flip confirmed at the close and exited at market. Their difference is the cost of confirmation, it is never zero, and that is why there is no blended figure here. R against designed risk divides by the SL distance the trade was sized for; R against risk parked divides by the stop SAR actually had out, which has been the wider of the two on most handovers. Both are defensible, so both are printed.

These arms are kept in their own ledger, separate from the live SAR arms. Every arm there belongs to a signal a subscriber received; every arm here belongs to one nobody received. Pooling them would inflate the population an adoption decision reads.
